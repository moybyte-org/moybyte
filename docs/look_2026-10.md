# The 2026-10 look: the build plan (2026-10-15)

The target is `docs/media/look_2026-10/`: `DESIGN.md` is the look,
`THEMES.md` the theme model and each theme's fields, `GAPS.md` what the
engine lacks and what each gap costs, `index.txt` names every mockup and
`draw.py` draws them. This doc orders that work into steps and copies
none of it; where they disagree, `DESIGN.md` wins. The look replaces D1 of
`docs/theming_2026-09.md` §1; the home is `docs/shell_layout_2026-10.md`
§12's component, restyled. Numbers live in **#66** (frames, the T-Deck and
S3 boards) and **#58** (the P4 boards).

## Decisions

- **Taste is the orchestrator's** (owner, 2026-10-15); engine and system
  changes are welcome for the look.
- **The rendering is modern, and the engine a superset** (owner,
  2026-10-15): anti-aliased type pre-rasterised at 2 or 4 bpp, smooth
  shapes, soft shadows, native 1× on the big boards; every one of those is a
  theme's choice, and the pixel look stays expressible. The pixel round
  this replaces is `cb195ae9` (`docs/media/look_2026-10/pixel_round3/`).
- **The seed set is three themes** (owner, 2026-10-15): Moy, with a Day
  mode (light, the default) and a Night mode (dark), which were the
  Daylight and Moy Night themes until the owner made them one theme the
  same day; Blocks; and Contrast (dark and light). Every other seed is
  deleted at step 27; under the no-migrations rule a stored theme name that
  no longer exists reads as absent and resolves to the default. The theme
  keys are `moy`, `blocks` and `contrast`; a mode is the variant `light`
  (Day) or `dark` (Night).
- **The editors are restyled in today's layout** (owner, 2026-10-15): the
  tab row, PROJECTS, UNDO, REDO and PLAY, and the playtest window (steps 23
  and 24). The Studio's panes and presets (`docs/studio_2026-09.md`) are a
  later project.
- **Code syntax colours are theme roles** (decided by the orchestrator
  under the owner's delegation, 2026-10-15): theming's `code_*` content
  roles (the mockups' `syn.*`) leave the content exemption, and the
  readability check holds each to 4.5:1 on the code surface (step 23). This
  reverses `docs/studio_2026-09.md` §5.1 and §8 and theming §3's "syntax
  colours are content", each corrected there.
- **The ricing themes are not built in** (owner, 2026-10-15): Luna, Coast,
  Pico, Bench and Adw ship later as installable packages in the
  moybyte-org/carts repo (step 29).
- **Fonts are not economised** (owner, 2026-10-15); a board whose image
  cannot hold its set grows its partition or sheds weight, the Zero first.
- **The built-in themes' fonts stay in the firmware image** as frozen flash
  bytes (owner, 2026-10-15). A board nearing its headroom floor grows its
  app partitions (a repartition is accepted: dev boards hold nothing of
  value). Moving the set to the flash store frees nothing: seeds are packed
  in the image and inflated on first boot, and the fonts deflate only to
  about 60%, so the store copy would double the flash they use. A theme
  installed from the store brings its own font files in its package, as
  packages do.
- **Focus and current are theme properties** (owner, 2026-10-15: "make it a
  property, easy to change later"): `focus` draws by size class and
  `current` is a plate, a fill or a bar (`DESIGN.md`, the marks). Moy lifts
  its cards, edge-bars its rows, underlines its small parts and marks
  current with a `value` plate; Blocks and Contrast keep a ring on cards
  and `invert` elsewhere.
- **Button kinds replace per-app roles** (owner, 2026-10-15).
- **Theme colours are RGB** (2026-10-15, GAPS 1). This reverses
  `docs/theming_2026-09.md` §11's decline, which is corrected there. A
  cart's colour stays `& 63`.
- **Shadows and backdrops are measured on glass before anything depends on
  them** (step 8).
- **No board pass per step** (owner, 2026-10-15: "it's mostly UI"): the
  boards run at step 8 and at the phase gate, and nowhere between.
- **The public UI API is built as we go** (owner, 2026-10-15): step 11a
  builds its core and each screen step adds the widgets it needs
  (`docs/ui_api_2026-10.md`'s decisions).
- **Deferred:** a manifest description and genre, and a best-score verb
  (both moy-spec's); the details pane shows what GAPS 20 lists without them.
- **A new look is a new seed's data,** so `night` and the old seeds keep
  their pixels until step 27. A structural step (the marks, a screen's rows)
  re-baselines every config once, by name.

## How every step runs

Each step is one worktree (`tools/worktree.py new NAME`) from `dev`'s tip,
landed by cherry-pick as one commit that marks the step "Landed
YYYY-MM-DD" here, which the review kit reads. A parallel step re-bakes
`tests/shell_goldens/hashes.json` after its rebase, never merging it by hand.

**Gates, in order:** `make test` with the step's net,
`tools/preflight.sh --web`, the review. A step runs no board pass. The
boards run twice: at step 8, which also puts steps 4, 6 and 7's C on glass,
and at the phase gate. One board agent runs at a time.

**Perf.** A step's **perf** names the `tools/p4_bench.py` scenarios it
moves and the boards they run on. Steps 4, 6 and 7's run at step 8, every
other step's at the phase gate, each beside the same scenario on the plan's
base (`d1b251b6`), recorded in #66 or #58. 2 ms or more is signal; 3 ms or
more needs an explanation there that names the step; every idle scenario
paints zero; the P4 desk is bandwidth-bound, so a full-window pass is
measured there. Every build fails under its board's `headroom_floor_kib`
(board.toml).

**Review.** A reviewer with no context gets only the step's real renders,
each beside its target mockup. The renders are the host's, from step 1's tool
(`tools/look_shots.py OUT --step N`); glass renders join at the phase
gate's final review.
The reviewer passes the step or lists every difference; a difference stands
only if it is RGB565 quantisation, real data, or a deviation the commit
names with its reason. A failed step goes back to its worker, then to a
fresh reviewer; a dependent step waits for the pass. An engine step's
review judges only what the step moves.

## The steps

### Targets

**1. The review kit.** Landed 2026-10-15. `tools/look_shots.py OUT
[SCENE ...] [--step N] [--theme NAME] [--variant MODE]` sets up each
`index.txt` scene on the host from the workstation the shell goldens build
(Sky Run's carousel, Star Catcher's code and frog, Calc keyed through the
target's tape, Calc over Files), renders it at the mockup's size and theme,
and writes `mockup | render` pairs, 3× for 320×240, with an `index.txt` the
reviewer reads. A scene is built when the shell holds its theme and every
step that targets it carries its Landed mark; until then it is the nearest
current screen (a missing theme drawn with the current theme the kit's
`FALLBACK` names), marked "not built yet" with what is missing. **Target**
every PNG by name; **net** `tests/test_look_shots.py`: every scene has a
setup and plan steps, and a few scenes render and pair; **goldens** none.

**2. The remaining targets.** Landed 2026-10-15. `draw.py` draws, in
Moy's Day and Night, the screens the pixel round laid out: the handheld Settings,
Appearance and Calc; the desk's console home and
Appearance window; the home and desk over a wallpaper. Contrast's light
home and desk join them. The layouts are `pixel_round3/`'s; each PNG enters
`index.txt` under the scene names `tools/look_shots.py` already sets up.
The orchestrator passes each picture before the screen step that targets it
starts. **Net** `tests/test_look_shots.py`; **goldens** none.

### Engine: 3, 5 and 9 in parallel; 4 after 3; 6, 7 and 10 after 5

**3. The font file and the font set** (GAPS 2, 4). Landed 2026-10-15. MFNT version 2 (1, 2
and 4 bpp, per-glyph boxes, kerning) with its validator, `tools/make_font.py`,
the byte map, the ellipsis cut (`moy_text_cut_px`, beside
`moy_text_fit_px`), a `caption` role, `MOY_FONT_SLOTS` to 10, PSRAM copies
at load, and Plex (built as Day Sans: "Plex" is a Reserved Font Name), Inter,
Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and JetBrains Mono at their size sets with
`THIRD_PARTY.md` notices, each board image carrying its tier's set (it
stays there: the decision of 2026-10-15 above). Version 1 files still load. **Target**
`type_specimen.png`; **net** `tests/test_font_lane.py` rasters for every
bpp, a fuzz of the version 2 header and records, the Code tab's hit-tests at
the 7 px cell; **goldens** none (no seed names the fonts yet); **perf** each
board's image headroom from its build, the Zero's included.

**4. The glyph lane** (GAPS 3). Landed 2026-10-15. A version 2 font's
coverage blends its ink in `mg_glyph_cov`, channel by channel in canonical
RGB565 whatever the buffer's wire order (`moy_text_wire`, set by the
canvas): over a known solid background (`moy_text_paint`'s `bg` and its
box) a glyph inside the box and clear of the glyphs before it stores from a
ramp built once a pass and reads nothing; every other glyph reads and
blends. The text shadow is a first pass at an offset and an alpha. The
toolkit passes the field a row or a button has just filled, and
`ui.label` takes `bg` and `shadow`; a canvas's `print` reads. The lanes
are one C body under every tier, so the twins are its bindings (`moy_ui`'s
`label`, `text_wire` and `text_lanes`, the op count), and the
`tools/p4_conformance.py` case is `tests/on_glass.py`'s
`glyph_lane_matches_host` instead: a conformance cart prints in petme128
and its frames map to palette indices, which blended words are not.
**Target** `type_specimen.png`; **net** `tests/test_glyph_lane.py`
against a Python reference at 1, 2 and 4 bpp (both lanes, clipping,
camera, scales 1 and 2, the shadow, the cut, both wire orders, and the
boards' unix module drawing a fuzzed run to the host's bytes),
`tests/test_device_canvas_parity.py` and the font-lane rasters;
**goldens** none (no seed names a 2 or 4 bpp face); **perf** the S3 A/B
the chrome text lane is gated on (`--repaint editor:code` on tdeck and
guition_s3, a 1 bpp cut of the mono face beside its 2 bpp one), and a
text-over-backdrop pass on p4, both at step 8.

**5. RGB colours and theme fields** (GAPS 1). Landed 2026-10-15. Roles take `#rrggbb`,
quantised into indices 64–127; `PAL565` grows to 128 in every copy (host,
device, web, wasm, the board flush drivers); every role keeps its nearest
MOY64 index for `theme()`, compiled apps' `theme` imports and cart
canvases; other themes' previews are RGB565 bakes and take no slots.
§4.10's derivation and §7's check run on RGB. The loader takes the theme
model's fields (`THEMES.md`) with their defaults. `format` and the seed
version are bumped, and a `moy` seed (Day and Night) joins `night` with
golden configs at both sizes in Day, "the Moy Day configs", and in Night. **Target** the
colours of the `moy-light_*` and `moy-dark_*` PNGs; **net** `tests/test_gate_pal_sync.py` at 128,
the loader, derive and readability tests on RGB, a fuzz of the new fields,
and a test that the live row, content set and swatches fit 64 slots;
**goldens** the Moy Day and Night configs are added; **perf** `--store` and `--edit`
on tdeck and p4, `tools/p4_conformance.py` on glass.

**6. Shapes, shadows, paints and bevels** (GAPS 5–8). Landed 2026-10-15.
Anti-aliased and
stepped corners, rings, per-corner radii and circles; soft and hard
shadows, which the WM casts under windows and the carousel under its hero
card for a theme that sets `shadow`; smooth, stepped and dithered paints;
bevel edges. As built: one kernel, `native/moy_gfx/moy_shape.c` (the canvas
verbs `rrect` and `shadow`, and the toolkit's), and the theme's depth
(`ui.use_depth`, `native/moy_ui/moy_ui_depth.c`) that draws the window frame
of a theme whose look is nearer than any frame style. A window's shadow and
its outline's fringe are drawn by the desk layer over what it has just
repainted (`runtime/wm_desk.py`), so nothing blends twice; a dragged window
casts `ws.wm.drag_shadow`'s size (smaller by default); the content keeps
clear of the rounded bottom corners by a foot; the hero casts only at rest.
Keys take their shape with their kinds (step 10). **Target**
`calc_all_themes.png`'s windows and keys, and the
windows of the `moy-light` and `moy-dark` desktops; **net**
`tests/test_shapes.py` (each primitive against a Python reference),
`tests/test_depth_shell.py` (the WM and the hero, counted),
`tests/test_device_canvas_parity.py`, `tests/test_gfx_binding.py`'s board
compilation and a `tools/p4_conformance.py --shapes` case
per primitive; **goldens** the Moy Day and Night configs; **perf** `--painter` on
tdeck and p4, and `--shadows` at step 8.

**7. Backdrops** (GAPS 9). Landed 2026-10-15. The `backdrop` field
rasterised into the one wallpaper bake, with its host and web twins and the
loader's caps: the loader reads `backdrop.home` and `backdrop.desk` into
records (`moy_theme_backdrop`), one C kernel draws them on every tier
(`native/moy_gfx/moy_backdrop.c`, `DeviceCanvas.backdrop`), and the
wallpaper rasters them once per theme load and mode switch into the
picture's bake (`Wallpaper._draw_backdrop`). A theme with a backdrop and no
wallpaper of its own shows it in place of the default wallpaper cart.
**Target** the backdrops of the `moy-light`, `moy-dark` and
`blocks-light` PNGs; **net** a raster test per shape kind, the loader fuzz
in `tests/test_theme_loader.py`; **goldens** the Moy Day configs; **perf**,
at step 8, `home idle` and `desk idle` paint zero on tdeck and p4.

**8. The first measurement on glass,** before any part or screen draws a
shadow or a backdrop. Three scenarios, each new in `tools/p4_bench.py`
(the backdrop's two are `--backdrop`, written at step 7):
a window drag on p4 and guition_p4 with Moy's Day and Night soft
shadows, beside no shadow and a drag-time smaller blur; the backdrop raster
on theme load and on a mode switch on p4; the T-Deck carousel over a
backdrop, with the hero's shadow in the card layer (the window drag and the
carousel are `tools/p4_bench.py --shadows`). The numbers go to #58
and #66, and the commit records the verdict: the shadow while a window
moves, and where a board keeps no full bake. The same pass runs steps 4,
6 and 7's **perf**, their `tools/p4_conformance.py` cases on glass, and
`tools/board.py pass tdeck p4`. **Target** none; **net** the
scenarios run on the host bench; **goldens** none.
Landed 2026-10-16, on all four console boards; the numbers are #66's and
#58's step-8 comments. The verdict:
- **The soft shadow while a window moves is affordable on both P4s, and the
  smaller drag shadow stays the default** (`ws.wm.drag_shadow = 1`): it
  costs a little over none, and the theme's full shadow twice that. Most
  of a Moy drag's cost over a theme without depth is the still windows'
  shadows and fringes, which the restore uncovers, plus the restore's
  larger rect. No drag size removes that, and clipping the shadows to the
  restored strips alone buys nothing.
- **The backdrop raster is acceptable.** It is one frame at a theme load
  and at a mode switch, and nothing rasters while the home moves. The
  T-Deck and the Waveshare P4 hold the full bake. The fallback that draws
  the backdrop each paint, where no bake can be had, did not run on glass.
- **The carousel holds** over a backdrop with the hero's shadow, and its
  idle paints zero. Step 6 is the only look step that moves the T-Deck's
  home, and it moves it by less than the 3 ms bar.
- **The AA text lane is acceptable on the S3s.** A 2 bpp mono face costs
  little over a 1 bpp cut of it on a full Code-tab repaint, and both beat
  the built-in face. Text over a backdrop on the P4 costs about as much.
- **Image headroom is what binds next on the P4s:** the font set and
  steps 4–7 left each under 100 KB above its floor.

**9. Native 1× on the big boards, and the 2× option** (GAPS 10, 11).
Landed 2026-10-16.
Per-class metrics, `chrome.text_scale_floor` answering 1 on the P4 boards,
Files, Get Carts, the Storybook and the editors on line metrics, default
window sizes re-planned, and `render.scale: 2` drawing the desk at 512×300.
The classes are `runtime/layout_base.py`'s `CLASSES`: a declared glass of
5" or more is a desk (bar 40, list rows 28, Places 34, title strips 44,
desk tiles 44 on a 74 pitch), its other chrome at a unit of 2, so the P4s
run chrome size 1 and CHROME SIZE offers 1 and 2. Line metrics keep every
offset petme128 and Tiny5 drew at and grow with a taller or wider face;
the Storybook's page words draw in the `mono` font, whose cells their caret
counts. `render.scale: 2` is a desk's alone: the shell draws on a
half-size layer at the desk's metrics halved, one `blit565` scale-2 pass a
drawn frame doubles it onto the glass (the PPA doubling GAPS 11 costs is
not wired), and the pointer lands in its pixels (`moy_input_ptr_t.div`).
**Target** the desk PNGs' geometry; **net** `tests/test_native_desk.py`,
`tests/test_files_app.py` and the editors' layout tests; **goldens** the
handheld configs hold, the P4 windowed configs re-baseline once, and
`guition_p4_1280x800_windowed` and `p4_1024x600_render2_moy_day` are added;
**perf** `tools/p4_bench.py --desk-scale` (`desk idle, three windows`,
`window drag (settings)` and `files scroll`, each at 1× and 2×) on p4 and
guition_p4.

**10. Button kinds** (GAPS 13). Landed 2026-10-15. `primary`, `tonal`, `toggle` beside
`normal` and `danger`; every caller in the tree (Python, Lua and the wasm
`ui_kind` names) moves to the new names in the same commit; per-kind
styling and `app_buttons`. style.json's `button_kinds` are the theme
model's five, drawn as `button`, `button_primary`, `button_tonal`,
`button_danger` and `cell`; each kind's colours derive from the base roles
(DESIGN.md), and a kind whose derived ink misses the floor on its field
takes its `floors` form (tonal: the normal ink; danger: the danger fill).
`ui.use_buttons` installs the live theme's `buttons` and `app_buttons`
(colours placed in the block, a paint fill its first stop) for
`moy_ui_button_style`; `ui.button(..., app=)` picks an app's overrides.
A compiled run's buttons take the style alone. `shape`, `r` and `bevel`
draw through step 6's rounded rects (`moy_ui_draw.c`'s `button_body`): a
theme with a look rounds its keys at `key_r` and bevels them under
`edges: bevel`, and a theme with none keeps them square, its surfaces as
they were. `font` resolves and does not draw: no type role is a key's or
an operator's.
`ui.button_unreadable` reports a kind under the floor; a second primary
on one canvas in a frame is warned of, and the goldens hold none.
**Target** `calc_all_themes.png`; **net**
`tests/test_ui_style_data.py` (one `primary` per surface, the readability
warning), Calc's tests; **goldens** every config that shows a button, one
named re-baseline; **perf** `app idle: calc` on tdeck and p4.
